# Exact arithmetic-matroid signatures from lattice realizations

Claim: ABC-ARITHMETIC-SIGNATURE.

## Domain and statement

The executable domain is an essential integral realization
$A\in\mathbb Z^{r\times e}$ in the explicitly declared ambient lattice
$\mathbb Z^r$: A has full row rank. It need not be TU or surjective.
Rank zero and loop columns are included. Nonessential matrices are
rejected rather than silently reduced or saturated.

For every subset X of the labeled columns, let

$$
H_X=A_X\mathbb Z^X,\qquad
\overline H_X=(H_X\otimes\mathbb Q)\cap\mathbb Z^r.
$$

The represented rank and multiplicity are

$$
r(X)=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}(A_X),\qquad
m(X)=|\operatorname{Tor}(\mathbb Z^r/H_X)|
     =[\overline H_X:H_X].
\tag{1}
$$

If a verified Smith certificate gives

$$
P A_X Q=D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_k,0,\ldots),\quad
P,Q\text{ integral unimodular},\quad
0<d_1\mid\cdots\mid d_k,
$$

then

$$
r(X)=k,\qquad m(X)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}d_i.
\tag{2}
$$

For k=0, the empty product is one. Comparing (r(X),m(X)) for **every**
subset, under a prescribed label bijection, is exact equality of the
represented arithmetic-matroid data. It does not assert equivalence of
realizations or invariance of their bicycle modules.

## 1. Smith coordinates prove the index formula

Changing generators by Q leaves their generated subgroup unchanged.
The ambient automorphism P carries H_X and its saturation to those of D.
In Smith coordinates the subgroup is generated by $d_i e_i$ for
$1\le i\le k$, and its saturation is generated by $e_i$ at those positions.
Their index is the product in (2).

The quotient is the direct sum of $\mathbb Z/d_i$ and the free group
$\mathbb Z^{r-k}$. Its torsion has that same order. This proves both
equalities in (1) and the rank assertion. In particular, dependent
subsets have a positive index; their multiplicity is not a zero
maximal determinant.

An empty subset or a subset of zero columns has rank zero and
multiplicity one. The ambient group is free, so m(empty)=1.
Surjectivity additionally requires m(E)=1; it is not implied by
torsion-freeness. The domain and definitions are audited in
[sources/arithmetic-matroid-background.md](../sources/arithmetic-matroid-background.md).

## 2. The independent determinantal-divisor route

For k>0, the gcd of all k-by-k minors of A_X is the product in (2).
Integral unimodular row and column transformations preserve the ideal
generated by those minors: multilinearity, or the minor form of
Cauchy–Binet, expresses transformed minors as integer combinations of
the original ones; the inverse transformations give the reverse
containment. For a rank-k Smith diagonal, the only nonzero k-minor is
the product of its k positive entries.

More generally, gcds of all j-minors give $d_1\cdots d_j$ for j<=k.
Their consecutive ratios recover the Smith factors. Vanishing minors
beyond k detect rank. This route uses Bareiss determinants and no
candidate Smith reduction, modular field ranks, or floating point.

## 3. Certificate verification and Euclidean construction

A certificate is accepted only after checking dimensions, strict integer
entries, unit determinants of P and Q, the complete identity P A_X Q=D,
diagonality, positive dividing factors, and trailing zeros.

The candidate construction tracks elementary integer row and column
operations. At each pivot it applies Euclidean division to clear the
pivot row and column. A nonzero remainder moves to the pivot and
strictly decreases its absolute value.

After the row and column are clear, a lower-block entry not divisible
by the pivot is brought into the pivot row. Further Euclidean steps
then produce a strict decrease. Between decreases, clearing has only
finitely many row and column entries to process. Once the pivot divides
the remaining block, it is made positive and fixed. Subsequent integer
operations preserve this divisibility and the earlier diagonal blocks.
Induction on the block size gives a dividing Smith diagonal.

Every operation is unimodular; no division by a nonunit in a residue
ring occurs. Construction and acceptance are separate: the returned
certificate is checked even after the reduction has finished.

## 4. Complete labels and canonical encodings

A subset is encoded by an integer mask whose bit j selects column j.
A prescribed permutation p is interpreted as target label j mapping
to source label p[j]. Thus target mask X compares with source mask p(X).

The versioned encoding records r, e, and the rank/multiplicity pair
for every mask in increasing order. Multiplicities are positive decimal
strings, so the fixture with an index exceeding 2^80 remains exact.
Here r is the total matroid rank
because the realization is essential. Matrix entries and Smith basis
choices are omitted. Exact equality compares the full data, not only
a digest, full-set Smith form, Gram determinant, or a selected basis.

These data are invariant under integral unimodular ambient changes and
column sign changes; a prescribed relabeling reindexes them.
Changing the ambient lattice by saturation or rational rescaling is
not one of these allowed operations and may change multiplicities.

This API only constructs signatures from validated realizations. It
does not accept an arbitrary rank/multiplicity table as proof that an
abstract arithmetic matroid is representable.

## 5. Independent modular cut membership

For every integer n>=2 and integral edge representative b,

$$
b\bmod n\in\operatorname{im}(A^\top\bmod n)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
b\in M:=A^\top\mathbb Z^r+n\mathbb Z^e.
\tag{3}
$$

M contains $n\mathbb Z^e$, so it has full rank. For $N=M+\mathbb Zb$,
the finite-index identity

$$
[\mathbb Z^e:M]=[\mathbb Z^e:N]\,[N:M]
$$

shows that b is in M exactly when the two ambient indices agree.
Both are gcds of full-size minors, of $[A^\top\mid nI]$ and its
augmentation by b. An independent oracle enumerates edge vectors,
retaining those with this cut condition and Ab=0 modulo n.
This is valid over arbitrary composite moduli and uses no potential
enumeration or unproved Gram torsion formula.

## 6. Scope after the counterexample

The [rank-two counterexample](arithmetic-realization-counterexample.md)
has equal complete signatures but different bicycle orders. Arithmetic
equality is therefore not a bicycle-equivalence certificate.
Even the isomorphism classes of every subset's integer quotient agree
in that pair; the row and column transformations are not interchangeable
with equality of those data.

The equality gate supports reproducible counterexamples and future
refinement work. It does not prove a new universal realization-invariance
claim. The separate integral proof is authority; candidate Python and
finite comparisons are implementation evidence.
